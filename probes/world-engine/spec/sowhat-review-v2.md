# So-what v2: independent review (2026-10-06)

**What this is.** A check of the shipped v2 composer (`demo/static/sowhat.js`, tables `rules_v2/sowhat2_*.csv`, backend `sowhat_story.py` and the v2 fields in `world_service2.py`) on stories nobody tuned against. Part A: 30 new stories run through a fresh engine. Part B: the 120 harness stories re-measured on the current composer against the targets in design section 13. Part C: the worst failures, who owns each one, and a verdict. No code or data was changed.

**What it is measured against.** Part A judges each statement against the numbers in its own engine response (readings, places, if-true, rank, size tier), not against the real world. Where the numbers themselves are odd (guessed decision weights), that is said and not counted against the composer, except when a composer rule makes the oddity louder (a causal join, a figure). Part B uses the saved engine responses in `scratchpad/diversity2/` and the tables served live by the engine (`GET /sowhat`: 11 frames, 82 hooks, 100 templates, 462 + 133 lexicon rows, checked equal to the CSVs).

Files: `scratchpad/review2/` (`stories.json`, `rNN.resp.json`, `compose.js`, `composed.json`, `composed.txt`; `partb/` holds copies of the harness scripts adapted to write there, plus `test1.json`, `test2.json`, `metrics.json`, `extras.json`, `ifcheck.js`). The engine ran on :8142 with `WE_RULES=rules_v2` and was stopped at the end.

### Judged by criterion

1. **Accuracy.** No statement has the wrong direction against its own response's numbers, except the inverted `l2_who` band (C1). Hedges are right on every forecast and opinion: modal on the trigger and theme 1, "would" on the A4 figure, and "could" made to agree ("who otherwise would not" never appears after "could" in this set). The overclaims come from somewhere else. A place the engine cannot resolve becomes a worldwide statement (r12, r29, r30; C2). The entry country contradicts the story text (r20). Causal joins ("so", "which means") assert links the engine only weights (r15, r21). The STAKE hook fires at 0.76 points (r17).
2. **Anchor integrity.** All 11 world anchors (and the 26 over all foci) trace to the lead decision's peak at the response row, in the right run (A4 if-true for hedged stories, A1 otherwise), rounded as specified, at or above 2.0. Degree words agree with the figure (17.1) in every case. The problems are in the wording and the hover, below.
3. **Language.** The new defects are verb agreement after "1" ("about 1 more cut spending", "roughly 1 more cut spending"; 4 statements here, 3 in the 120 set), the same act said twice (r04), and two unconnected line 2 fragments ("Well above what most people do. Sharp at first, then mostly gone."). One stock run is still there: "almost alone" (r21), "carries this one alone", "Only X feels this", which give three "alone" leads in a row in triple 19-21. The trace has a typo: every A4 trace reads "itself against **theno-event** control run" (missing space in `aTrace`).
4. **Honesty on quiet, mixed and forecast stories.** Quiet stories are honest and varied (r08, r09, r14, r22, r23, r25, r26). Forecasts say what would happen if true, and r01, r02, r03 and r04 get figures where v1 would have gone quiet. Mixed stories: r19 is right at both foci, r11 carries its other side, and r20 loses its other side at world focus to the shrink order (C3). r21's other side (rents up) is never shown, because the partner test needs the opposite valence and rents are the same valence as the lead.
5. **Three-things test** (10 random consecutive triples of world statements, seed 7, starting at r02, r03, r04, r05, r11, r12, r13, r18, r19, r21). 6 sound different, 2 partly, 2 do not. **r02-r04**: two of three open "On this forecast, X would ..." (IF03 twice), and r02 and r03 both end "3 in every 100 people would cut spending". **r19-r21**: three local "alone" leads in a row, two ending with an "in every N people in X cut spending" line 2. The partly cases are r03-r05 (three conditional "would" statements with the same rhythm) and r12-r14 (two "X: people cut/rein in spending" heads). Same conclusion as the harness: different words, but the shape still repeats within IF and WHO_LOCAL.

### Anchor: is it honest enough for a non-expert?

**The number is honest. The sentence and the hover, as written, are not yet.**

- *Traceable:* yes. Every digit is the rounded peak of one decision at one row, against the no-event control run.
- *Could be read as a headcount over a time window we do not have:* yes. "About 20 in every 100 people cut spending who otherwise would not" (r28) and "Out of every 10 people in Saudi Arabia, roughly 1 more cut spending than without it" (r20) read as counts of real people who did something within some period. What the number is: the gap between two simulated runs, in points of likelihood of one decision, at whichever of the three checkpoints (Now = 3 ticks, Next, Later) the gap is largest. The engine's own note says the tick-to-day mapping is UNCLEAR (`world_service2.py` line 195: Next is about a month and Later about five months *if* one tick is a day). The sentence never says it is a model estimate, never says when, and "cut spending" stands for a shift in the spend-versus-save choice.
- *What the hover says today:* `Number: about 7 in every 100 people ...` followed by `A1: spend -6.87 pts at World (peak at Next), vs the no-event control run; shown as 7 (round half up); threshold 2.0`. It names what the figure is measured against, but in jargon (A1, pts, control run, threshold). It never defines Next. It does not say "model estimate". For forecasts it reads "vs the if-true run with the type weight removed, itself against theno-event control run".
- *Degree word against figure:* consistent everywhere (17.1 holds). The 1-in-20 and 1-in-10 bands are right on the value. With n=1 the verb does not agree.
- *Amplification:* for a forecast, the A4 figure is about 3.3 times the weighted value (the if-true run removes the 0.3 type weight). That is correct by design, but the "if so" can be lost on a reader. r02 turns a coffee forecast into "3 in every 100 people" worldwide.

**Hover wording I would ship** (built from fields the response already has; `{…}` are slots):

> **Number: a model estimate, not a count.** In our simulated population, about {n} more people in every {unit} {act} in the run with this story than in the same run without it. This is the largest gap across our three checkpoints (Now, Next, Later), reached at {Next}; Next is roughly a month after the news if one model step is a day, and that time scale is our guess. It rests on hand-set decision weights, and a figure is shown only when the gap is at least 2 in 100.

For a hedged story, add: *"This assumes the {forecast} comes true. Counted as a {forecast}, the engine gives it {0.3} of that weight."* For the 1-in-{20,10} bands, add: *"({value} in 100, shown as 1 in {unit})."* Keep the current technical trace under a "details" fold, with the missing space fixed. In the sentence itself, the anchor rows that end "than without it" could end "than without it, on our model" when the anchor is in line 2, where "it" otherwise has nothing to refer to. That is optional. The hover is the required part.

### Ordered fix list

1. **C1**: in `ratio()`, treat a reference under 0.25 points as "the world barely moves": kind `x_many` when the row is at least 0.25 (or no `l2_who` at all). Add a unit test with an exact 0 world value.
2. **C4 hover**: replace the anchor hover with the wording above (model estimate, the checkpoint and the stated time-scale guess, the if-true premise, the 2-point floor). Fix the "theno-event" space. Make the verb agree after "1" (an `act_1` form, or "one more person cuts").
3. **C2**: when `entry.place` is `unresolved` and the story is counted, do not speak for "people" worldwide. Either use a quiet-style form that says the place is outside our map, or carry a hook that says so and never show a figure. The backend should also resolve two-country stories to the first named country, or send both.
4. **C3**: keep the partner class in the "shortest body" and "shortest hook" steps (shortest *among* partner bodies first). When the anchor and a partner compete for line 2, prefer the partner and drop the figure.
5. **C5**: add `needs = causal_ok` to "so", "which means", "in turn", "leaving": the lead theme must be spending or travel and the trigger must have a good/bad valence. Otherwise use the plain join. Raise the STAKE gate to the 1-point "clear" floor (wording, not weights).
6. Tables: two more announced_w IF rows; vary the second act verb when the theme verb already says it (r04); retire "almost alone" from WL02 or give it alternates.

None of these needs a change to the guessed decision weights.

### Verdict

**Ship after 3 fixes: C1, the C4 hover, and C2.** The targets pass, except hook evenness by row (IF01 37.5%, a table-only fix). Figures are faithful, hedges are right, and quiet stories are honest and varied. But on fresh stories, 8 of 30 have at least one wrong statement, 3 of them from a single `ratio()` bug, and the anchor as shown would be read by a non-expert as a count of real people over some period. C3 and C5 should follow soon after. They are wrong in the reader's eyes, but rarer.
