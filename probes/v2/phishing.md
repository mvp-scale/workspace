# phishing.jsonl

Builder: `build_phishing.py` (seed 20260921). Retrieved 2026-09-21.

- Source: Hugging Face `ealvaradob/phishing-dataset`, file `texts.json` (email and SMS portions; URLs and HTML not used), snapshot 55caf9af. https://huggingface.co/datasets/ealvaradob/phishing-dataset . Licence: Apache-2.0 (dataset card). Upstream components: Kaggle "phishingemails" mail set, Mendeley SMS phishing set (Mishra & Soni), and, judging by content, SpamAssassin/Enron/Nazario style mail and the UCI SMS Spam Collection. The card documents only some of this; provenance of individual rows is not recorded.
- Task: noul, "Is this message trying to get the recipient to hand over credentials, money or access through impersonation, pressure or deception?" yes = upstream label 1, no = label 0.
- Counts: 64 items, yes 32 / no 32; each class 16 short (<=300 chars, SMS-like) + 16 long (email-like), seeded stratified.
- Cleaning: whitespace normalised; dropped texts with mojibake or the words spam/ham/phish/smish; truncated at a sentence boundary under 1200 chars (max state length 1193); minimum 40 chars.
- **Spam versus phishing.** Upstream label 1 lumps phishing, smishing and plain spam. An unfiltered draft was mostly drug/mortgage/stock ads. I therefore applied a mechanical keyword filter to the positive class only (credential, verify, login, account, prize/claim, inheritance/beneficiary, etc. required; ad terms such as unsubscribe, mortgage, pharmacy, penny stock excluded). Labels were never edited. This makes positives more phishing/scam-like and selection-biased toward keyword cues; a model may score well on those cues. Negatives are unfiltered, and are mostly Enron business email, linguistics mailing-list posts, news digests and casual SMS, so they are easy and stylistically different (many emails are lowercased and tokenised with spaces around punctuation, which correlates with source rather than label).
- Label noise: I read a random sample of 20 items (seed 5). About 3 of 20 (15%) looked mislabelled or off-question: an eBay income-scheme advert, a stock-pump email, and a "Sawan ki Bahar" agent competition SMS labelled phishing. Prize/lottery SMS (UCI-style premium-rate scams) count as scams, not credential phishing, but fit "money through deception". No negatives looked wrong.
- Contamination: Enron, SpamAssassin, UCI SMS Spam and Nazario corpora are ubiquitous in public training data.
