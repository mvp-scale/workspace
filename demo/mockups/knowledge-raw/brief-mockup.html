Why a ladder instead of fixed cards

The old card had fixed slots (role, did, because, allies, opponents, effect). It filled them the same way whether we'd seen a name once or followed it for four days. The reader couldn't tell how much we actually know, what that knowledge rests on, or what's still missing.

Knowledge builds up in order. First you know what something is and what it did. Then you follow how it unfolds. Only then can you say what to expect. The ladder puts each concept on the step it has actually reached and names the next question. When a new article arrives, you can see whether it moved something up a step or only repeated what we had.

Why three steps, not five: we measured five on the real data (measure-ladder.py). A separate "why" step would read "not read" for 97–100% of concepts. Disputes and stated causes also turn up in the first article, before there's any storyline. So "why" and "disputed" are kinds of line inside FOLLOW, not steps of their own.

The steps, and the goal of each

1 · KNOW

What is it, and what did it do?
Goal: one fact you can remember. The name, its kind, and two or three things it did, each with a date and how many outlets.
  • Retention: what a reader should be able to rebuild from memory.
  • Built from: type, outside definition (Wikidata) if we have one, quoted action sentences.
  • Reached by: c1 68%, mix 45% of concepts.

2 · FOLLOW

How is it unfolding, why, and who disagrees?
Goal: the story in order. Dated steps joined by after, because and but.
  • Retention: what a reader re-reads when they come back.
  • Built from: deep timeline grouped by day, stated causes, speaker-pair disputes.
  • Reached by: c1 55%, mix 26% (steps on 2+ days).

3 · EXPECT

What comes next, and what should I watch?
Goal: a forward statement from a named speaker that a later article can confirm or contradict. If there is none, the concept reads as settled.
  • Growth: the line tomorrow's news tests.
  • Built from: future-tense sentences with a speaker.
  • Reached by: c1 8%, mix 6% attributed. Thin, so it's kept as its own step and its empty state is shown honestly.

Evidence (go deep)

Show me where that came from.
Goal: trust. Every line above opens its sentences, with publisher, day and fact id.
  • Not a step: every line points here.
  • Built from: deep timeline (100% coverage).
  • Left out on purpose: "accounts" and "history" (noise, see gaps).
Two more parts on every concept: Next question, the first step whose test is still empty, so you know what to look for. Since your last look, which steps or certainty words changed; repeats collapse to a count.
Steps never drop. If a denial arrives, the line changes its word (established → disputed) and is marked "changed since your last look". No banner.

Gaps: what the ladder needs that we don't have yet

1
Cause goes to the wrong concept. The necropsy result sits on Cincinnati Zoo, not Fiona. "causing/caused" isn't detected. Some "because" clauses attach to the wrong concept. Cheapest real fix.
2
Disputes quote the wrong text. They should use each side's own quote. A speaker contradicting itself (Saudi "respond") isn't a dispute.
3
EXPECT needs a speaker and a future verb (will, would, plans to). The speaker field already exists on 48 (c1) and 28 (mix) facts.
4
The "did" text is often broken ("carry out Yemen"). Show the quoted sentence until the line-vs-source check (R1/R2) passes.
5
The deep "accounts" and "history" sections are noise. Use the deep timeline only. "Direction" is effectively empty, so it isn't a step.
6
No storylines yet. The ladder lives on the concept until R1 splits hubs into storylines.
