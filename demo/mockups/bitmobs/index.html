How an article is read

The goal is an engine that reads news and builds its own training data. Cheap tools read each article first and keep everything they can prove. Each BitMob then learns from what the tools found, and its answers are checked against outside labels afterwards. Click any line to see numbers and examples.

Combined, the views do not beat the Reader. A weighted vote scores 0.354, against 0.438 for the Reader alone. Adding entity names to the Reader gives 0.452, headline words 0.449, body words 0.448; adding structure counts lowers it to 0.394. These gains are small.

Why. The combiner judged body words 0.88 reliable; on human labels they score 0.36. Every trained view learned from the same seeds, so their mistakes are the same mistakes and agreement is not independent evidence. Independent evidence has to come from sources that do not share a teacher: the publisher, the neighbours, the links.

The class definitions. Tool-built labels reach about 0.45 against the human 17-class convention; a word model trained on those human labels reaches 0.80 (ledger L1028). The tools do not know where a human draws the line between, say, society and human interest. How few human-labelled examples fix that is not measured for 17 classes.

7. How far does the lazy loop go?

One tool (the URL section) labelled articles as they were read, a model retrained after every 10,000, and the result was scored on 10,916 human-labelled articles. It levelled off at 0.32, because 5 of 17 classes never got a label and sport made up 44% of the labels. Each retrain took 9 seconds. That is the limit of one tool of the seven, not of the loop.
