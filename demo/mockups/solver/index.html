The BitMob Solver: how an article becomes a Pulse answer

An article comes in. Tools read what the text proves. A small model answers what is left. The teacher labels the rest, and that trains the next model. Built: the tools, one model, the teacher and the scoring. Missing: the step that decides what to improve next.

Act 1

The route

Six steps. Each hands a checked result to the next, and the last one gives the answer with its working.

The BitMob Solver is the whole chain: fixed-rule tools and BitMob models working together. An article passes through six steps. Each step hands a checked result to the next. The rules decide when a model is worth calling. The chain is defined in the playbook (sections 1, 2, 4) and built as analyze2.py.

The BitMob toolchain: what each tool does, and whether it earns its place

The six steps above are groups. This is the actual list, grouped by job. Three words used in the table:

Live
The tool runs when we ask the Solver to answer one article (the path in analyze2.py). If a tool is not live, it does not affect any answer we give today.
Harvest
The tool runs in the batch job that reads thousands of articles to collect evidence and training labels (harvest.py, extract.py). It feeds training, not answers.
Neither
Built and tested, but not part of either path yet.

Rule: a tool stays in the toolchain only with a stated point and a measured answer to "does it add value". 7 add value, 7 are weak or mixed, 8 are not measured yet. "Adds value" means a measured gain against an outside answer or against the same step switched off. Passing our own checks is not truth, so those are marked weak. Where a tool is not live, the table says why, or says that no reason is recorded. Of 22 tools, 14 are live and 6 more are harvest only. Words

What the terms mean

BitMob toolchain
The set of tools that read the text with fixed rules, listed one by one in Act 1. Each has a point, a definition and a measured answer to "does it add value".
BitMob Solver
The whole chain: rules and tools plus BitMob models. Code: analyze2.py.
BitMob model
One of our small trained models that runs on a normal CPU.
Rules
Fixed instructions. Same input, same output.
Trained model
Learned from labelled examples. It can be wrong in ways nobody wrote down.
Label
The right answer for one example, from a person or from Claude.
Stated fact
A fact written in the text that passed all four checks.
Abstain
Decline to answer, and say why.
