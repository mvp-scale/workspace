The Mob solver: how an article becomes a Pulse answer

The Mob solver is the whole chain: fixed-rule tools and BitMob models working together. An article passes through six steps. Each step hands a checked result to the next. The rules decide when a model is worth calling. The chain is defined in the playbook (sections 1, 2, 4) and built as analyze2.py.

Sources: analyze2.py; facts_lib.py; runs/pulse/timing_analyze2.json#t8_reader_median, #t8_solver_median (median, 8 threads); runs/pulse/rust_decider_flat.json#ns_per_call. Times not shown are not measured.

Step 6 is tested by the 160 cases in vectors.txt, made from the Lean rules. The Rust build fails if any case differs. Steps 1 to 5 are still Python.

A planned step in front

BitMob Keys (trained model, candidate, not registered) would read first and give the topic in about 1 ms. The BitMob Reader would only see what BitMob Keys won't answer. In a test on 1,600 human-labelled articles it answered 74.4% of them and was right within two categories on 96.0% of those. It is tested alone and not wired in.

Sources: runs/pulse/sparse_selective_flat.json#share_070, #right_070; runs/pulse/sparse_topic_human.json#word12_median_us

Does it improve itself?

Not yet. The loop is: answer, collect the answers it wasn't sure of, get them labelled, retrain, check, repeat. The Lean rule that decides which answers may become labels exists. The retraining and the drift check do not.

We have no settled name for this loop in our files.

Words

Mob solver
The whole chain: rules and tools plus BitMob models. Code: analyze2.py.
BitMob model
One of our small trained models that runs on a normal CPU.
Rules
Fixed instructions. Same input, same output.
Trained model
Learned from labelled examples. It can be wrong in ways nobody wrote down.
Label
The right answer for one example, from a person or from Claude.
Stated fact
A fact written in the text that passed all four checks.
Abstain
Decline to answer, and say why.
