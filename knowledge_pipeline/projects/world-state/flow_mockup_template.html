What the two deep-research returns told us, and where each idea stands

Two returns (one from OpenAI, one from Gemini, by file style). They agree on the order: fix the labels, add a gate, train a soft-label student. Neither has had its citations checked yet.

Teacher fusion with soft labelsQUEUEDBoth returns rank it first. Scored by agreement with a third system (local Clef), since no human key exists.
Label-error ranking (cleanlab idea)BUILTDone as T2 by hand; no install.

Patterns that worked or showed signal but are not used yet (to think divergently from)

Predict 'passing mention' directly.The teachers differ on whether a mention counts. A third output, 'is this the main subject?', has a measured signal already (AUC 0.757). We have treated this as noise to clean; it may be a label to learn.IDEA, UNTESTED
Route by disagreement, not by confidence.Spend Sonnet or Jev only where the teachers are likely to split (the T2 ranking), instead of on every item. Not wired in.IDEA, UNTESTED
Use source as a prior, not a feature.Condition rates range from 36.4% (DW) to 4.5% (Boston.com). As a plain feature it added +0.001, but a per-source threshold or base rate was never tried.IDEA, UNTESTED
Headline first.Headline words alone score 0.702 against 0.757 for everything. A headline-only fast pass might decide most items before reading the body.IDEA, UNTESTED
Skip the floor for free.15.6% of items have almost nothing identifiable and carry a condition 4.6% of the time. Marking them 'no condition' costs no model.IDEA, UNTESTED
Fingerprint hit-count as a second voter.Its AUC is 0.771, and it errs differently from the embedding. The two have not been combined.IDEA, UNTESTED
Outside data as an independent voter.The second teacher is not independent of the first. A labelled public set (GDELT codes, SemEval direction) would be.IDEA, UNTESTED
Rolling window.News drift costs about 0.015. A retrain on the latest items (Pass 5) is the direct fix.IDEA, UNTESTED

The words, in plain terms

Part
One small piece cut from an article: a word, a pair of words, a word from the headline, a type of name (place, person), a structure flag (has a quote, talks about the future), or the news source.
Outcome
A label a layer gave the article: "names a condition", story type, topic, "Sonnet agrees".
Significant part
A part that shows up with an outcome much more often than chance, measured over the whole collection and never one article.
Snapshot
A numbered, frozen copy of all part scores (v0, v1, v2…). The next batch is scored against the last snapshot before it is added, so we can see whether the knowledge is really improving, and roll back if it is not.
Floor
An article with almost no significant parts. We say "nothing identifiable" instead of forcing an answer.

The seven kinds of part, with a real example

Example article: France 24, 24 Oct 2026. Sentence: "The Ministry of the Interior estimates at least 410,000 people rallied across France for Saturday's protests against the high cost of living, including 110,000 in Paris." Labels belong to the whole article; parts are cut out of its text.

Not used: three-word phrases, who-did-what grammar, and meaning vectors (the embedding is a separate layer that could feed the same store). Parts are one lens, not the only method.
